# Luna's Bouquet — Voice Guide

## Feel
Warm, confident, quietly premium. A florist who takes her craft seriously and makes you feel
looked-after. Editorial, not salesy. Think a boutique florist's Instagram caption, not a chain's
promo blast.

## Do
- Lead with the flowers and the feeling, then the practical detail.
- Short, assured sentences. Let white space breathe like the arrangements do.
- Use Annie's story where it earns its place — on the About page — told with pride, not pity.
- Concrete, sensory language: "peonies at their peak," "wrapped and delivered to your door."

## Don't
- No pity framing around foster care. She's a talented young founder building something. Period.
- No exclamation-heavy hype. No "AMAZING DEALS."
- No em dashes in customer-facing marketing copy (Christine's standing rule). Use periods or commas.
- No AI-tell filler words ("quietly" as a crutch, "elevate," "nestled," "boasts").
